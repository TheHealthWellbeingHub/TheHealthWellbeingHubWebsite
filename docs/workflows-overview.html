The constraint that shapes all eight

HubSpot Starter allows one simple workflow per form, triggered by a form submission only — ten actions, no branching, no webhooks. Four of the eight workflows below have no form behind them, so each needs either a new form built on the site or automation that lives outside HubSpot entirely. That decision is the same decision eight times over, and it is worth settling once.

The automated front half works. The back half — stages four and five — does not exist yet, and it is the more valuable half: a referrer who never hears what became of their referral stops referring. It is also the first piece of real Claude-in-the-loop work in the whole register, rather than a HubSpot workflow.

Two older items are still open regardless: the endpoint hardening (CORS allowlist, honeypot, rate limiting), and the duplicate-contact bug — upsertContact dedupes on email only, so a participant referred with a phone number and no email creates a brand new contact on every single submission.

Next Define the outcome step: which three outcomes get recorded, where they are written, and what the referrer is told in each case.
Watch Telling a referrer that someone declined services shares a participant's decision with a third party. What can be said, and with whose consent, needs settling before this is automated.

The one that works end to end. Verified against production on 19 August 2026: endpoint → contact, deal, note, task → merge properties → Forms API enrolment → workflow → email sent and delivered. This is the working reference implementation, and every workflow below should be built to match its shape.

Three things stay open. The copy has never been reviewed — it is participant-facing and needs Kholoud. Inbox placement is unconfirmed: delivery proves the receiving server accepted the mail, not that it cleared spam. And first_response_at is not stamped, because the webhook action that would report an actual send does not exist on Starter.

Next Get the copy reviewed and approved, then confirm inbox placement with a real send to a few outside addresses.
03 Not started

New participant

Trigger
None — needs one
Email
04-participant-welcome-onboarding.html
Spec
Not written

The welcome and onboarding email is written and sitting in the library, with nothing to send it. This is the first workflow where the trigger is the whole problem: a participant becomes a participant when a service agreement is signed, which is a deal stage change, not a form submission — and Starter cannot trigger on deal stages.

So this needs a decision rather than a build. The realistic options are an internal form the team submits when onboarding someone, or driving the send from outside HubSpot. Whichever way this goes sets the pattern for 04, 05 and 08, which all have the same shape.

Next Decide the trigger mechanism once, for this and the three that follow it. Then write the spec.
04 Not started

Maintaining participants

Trigger
None — needs one
Email
No template exists
Spec
Not written

The only one of the eight with nothing behind it at all — no template, no spec, no trigger. It is also the only recurring one: the others fire once on an event, this runs on a rhythm.

What it should actually do is genuinely undecided. Plan review reminders as a plan end date approaches, periodic check-ins, service agreement renewals, and re-contacting participants who have gone quiet are all plausible readings, and they need different data and different cadences. Worth defining before anything is built.

Next Define what maintaining a participant means in practice — which moments deserve contact, and how often.
Watch Anything tied to plan dates or plan contents touches what a participant's plan will cover. That is compliance wording — drafted here, approved by a human.
05 Not started

Support worker introduction

Trigger
None — needs one
Email
06-support-worker-introduction.html
Spec
Not written

The template is written and expects a support worker's name, and probably a photo and a short introduction. None of that data exists in the CRM today — there are no support worker records, so there is nothing for the merge fields to read from.

This one matters more than its position suggests. Gender-matched workers and shared language are central to why a family chooses this provider, so the introduction is doing real work, not being polite. It is worth getting right rather than getting out.

Next Decide how support workers are represented in HubSpot before touching the email.

Template written, no trigger. This is the one workflow where the obvious source is not HubSpot at all — appointments live in a calendar, and Google Calendar is already connected to Claude in this workspace. A confirmation driven from a real calendar event is closer to how the work actually happens than one driven from a form.

It is also the only one with a natural second beat: a reminder before the appointment, not just a confirmation after booking. Worth deciding whether that is in scope now or later.

The complaints page is live and static. It explains how to complain, but there is no form on it — so there is nothing to capture a complaint, nothing to acknowledge, and no record that one was ever made. The email template exists and has never had a way to fire.

The build here is straightforward and mirrors the enquiry path exactly: a form, the endpoint handling a third form_name, a HubSpot form, and a workflow. Of the six not-started workflows, this is the one that is genuinely ready to build.

Next Build the complaints form and route it through the existing endpoint — the enquiry path is the template.

08 Not started

Service cancellation and exit

Trigger
None — needs one
Email
09-service-cancellation-exit.html
Spec
Not written

Closes the lifecycle. Template written, no trigger, and the same deal-stage problem as 03 — a service ending is a stage change, not a form submission. Whatever is decided for 03 applies here.

This is the most sensitive email of the eight. It reaches someone at the point they are leaving, sometimes unhappily, and it is the last thing they will remember. An automated send here can do real damage if the timing or tone is wrong, so it is worth asking whether this should be automatic at all, or a prompt for a person to send it deliberately.

Next Decide automated versus prompted, then follow whatever 03 settles for the trigger.
Watch Exit wording touches notice periods and service agreement terms. Compliance review before it sends to anyone.

07 — Feedback acknowledgement. The sibling of complaint acknowledgement, blocked by the same missing form. If a form is built on /complaints-feedback/ for workflow 07 above, this one comes almost free.
