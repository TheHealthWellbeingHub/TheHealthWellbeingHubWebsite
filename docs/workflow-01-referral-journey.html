What these two branches fix

Before this, a referral by email, phone, text or in person had no path at all. No record, no job, no clock, no acknowledgement to the person who referred them. It existed only in someone's inbox or memory.

The fix is deliberately small, and it is now live. The worker does not have to find a form, log into anything, or remember a process — they tell Claude there's a new referral, Claude hands back the form, they fill it in. From the moment it is submitted, the referral is indistinguishable from a website one: same records, same job, same two-hour clock, same acknowledgement email to the referrer.

That last point is what makes this worth doing. The support coordinator who phoned you gets the same “thanks, we've got it” email as one who used the website — and lands in the same reporting, so “who refers us the most” finally counts everyone.

Two fields the website form doesn't have

The staff form records how the referral arrived — phone, email, text or in person — and who on the team took it. Everything else on it is the public form, word for word.

A website referral records its channel too, as “Website form”. Without that, the reporting would only ever show the channels a worker typed in, which answers “where do our referrals come from” exactly backwards.

The three decisions behind it

Settled on 24 August 2026. Recorded here because each one is a judgement that someone will eventually want to re-open, and the reasoning is easier to argue with than to reconstruct.

Consent is attested, not copied

Nobody can read a privacy policy on someone else's behalf, so the worker does not tick the referrer's box. They tick their own: that they told the referrer we store their details, and where to find the Privacy Policy. Consent is still mandatory — a submission without it is refused, and sending the website form's tick instead does not get past it.

Because that is a different fact from a referrer ticking a box themselves, the record says which of the two it was. If consent is ever questioned, that distinction is the whole answer, and it cannot be reconstructed after the fact.

The link is the key

No login. A worker asks Claude and gets the address, and the page is not linked from anywhere on the site or offered to search engines. Chosen so nothing stands between a worker and the form while someone is waiting on the phone.

Worth being plain about the trade: this is obscurity, not a lock. Anyone holding the address can open it — the same as the public referral form, so it is not a step backwards, but the link should not be forwarded outside the team.

A separate submission limit for the office

The public form allows five submissions per ten minutes from one internet connection, which is right for one person filling in one form and wrong for a team sharing an office connection — the second worker of the morning would simply be blocked. Staff submissions get their own ceiling of forty. Still a limit, just a deliberate one.
