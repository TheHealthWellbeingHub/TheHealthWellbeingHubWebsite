What these two branches fix

Before this, a referral by email, phone, text or in person had no path at all. No record, no job, no clock, no acknowledgement to the person who referred them. It existed only in someone's inbox or memory.

The fix is deliberately small, and it is now live. The worker does not have to find a form, log into anything, or remember a process — they tell Claude there's a new referral, Claude hands back the form, they fill it in. From the moment it is submitted, the referral is indistinguishable from a website one: same records, same job, same two-hour clock, same acknowledgement email to the referrer.

That last point is what makes this worth doing. The support coordinator who phoned you gets the same “thanks, we've got it” email as one who used the website — and lands in the same reporting, so “who refers us the most” finally counts everyone.

Two fields the website form doesn't have

The staff form records how the referral arrived — phone, email, text or in person — and who on the team took it. Everything else on it is the public form, word for word.

A website referral records its channel too, as “Website form”. Without that, the reporting would only ever show the channels a worker typed in, which answers “where do our referrals come from” exactly backwards.

What happens after the call

The worker tells Claude how it went, in ordinary words. The outcome is written to the record every time — that part does not branch, because a call that happened and was never written down is the same as a call that never happened.

What differs is what comes next.

They're going ahead. Claude emails the referrer to say so — template 13, added 24 August 2026 to close a gap this page used to flag here: a referrer used to hear nothing when their own referral said yes. The deal moves to Service Agreement Sent — active, not yet onboarded. Actually sending the participant their own paperwork, the Consent email and later the Welcome email, is still a separate, deliberate step; nothing here starts that on its own.

They want time to think. Claude emails the referrer to say so, and that we will come back either way. It sets no deadline and asks nothing of them. Nothing has been decided, so the deal stage is left exactly where it was.

They said no. Claude emails the referrer to say the person is not going ahead, so the referrer can look elsewhere without waiting on us. No reason is given — not because we would not know, but because a participant does not owe anyone one, and passing it on would disclose more than the decision itself. The deal moves to Lost / Not Suitable — the only outcome that does, along with a service being cancelled later on.

All three emails live in the template library — 10, 11 and 13 — and the wording is approved. The email that sends is built from the template itself rather than retyped alongside it, so the approved wording is the wording that goes out.

How the outcome step actually works

Built and run end to end on 24 August 2026. It is worth being precise about what “built” means here, because it does not work like the rest of the page.

Nothing fires on its own. The steps above the trunk are automatic — a form is submitted and the system reacts. The outcome step has no trigger at all: the worker telling Claude is the trigger. That is deliberate, but it means if nobody says anything, nothing happens and no alarm sounds.

The email does not come from HubSpot. On Starter, workflows can only be triggered by a form submission — there is no way to fire one when a deal reaches “Lost”. Building this as a HubSpot email would have produced an asset nothing could trigger. It sends through the H&W mailbox instead, which also suits it better: this is a one-to-one message to one person, not a marketing send to a list.

Claude confirms before it sends. Every other step in this workflow writes a record you can correct. This one leaves the building. So Claude reads back which referral it has understood — participant, referrer, reference — and shows a rendered preview of the actual email, subject line and all, before it waits for a yes. Picking the wrong one would disclose the wrong person's decision to someone outside the service, and no amount of care afterwards undoes that.

What gets written: referral_outcome records which of the three it was, referral_outcome_date records the day of the conversation rather than the day it was typed, and a note keeps the worker's own words — all three, every time. The deal stage is the one thing that branches by outcome: Service Agreement Sent for going ahead, left untouched for wants time to think, and Lost / Not Suitable only for a no — never for a yes. Settled 24 August 2026, replacing an earlier version of this page that moved every outcome to Lost / Not Suitable, going ahead included.

The three decisions behind it

Settled on 24 August 2026. Recorded here because each one is a judgement that someone will eventually want to re-open, and the reasoning is easier to argue with than to reconstruct.

Consent is attested, not copied

Nobody can read a privacy policy on someone else's behalf, so the worker does not tick the referrer's box. They tick their own: that they told the referrer we store their details, and where to find the Privacy Policy. Consent is still mandatory — a submission without it is refused, and sending the website form's tick instead does not get past it.

Because that is a different fact from a referrer ticking a box themselves, the record says which of the two it was. If consent is ever questioned, that distinction is the whole answer, and it cannot be reconstructed after the fact.

The link is the key

No login. A worker asks Claude and gets the address, and the page is not linked from anywhere on the site or offered to search engines. Chosen so nothing stands between a worker and the form while someone is waiting on the phone.

Worth being plain about the trade: this is obscurity, not a lock. Anyone holding the address can open it — the same as the public referral form, so it is not a step backwards, but the link should not be forwarded outside the team.

A separate submission limit for the office

The public form allows five submissions per ten minutes from one internet connection, which is right for one person filling in one form and wrong for a team sharing an office connection — the second worker of the morning would simply be blocked. Staff submissions get their own ceiling of forty. Still a limit, just a deliberate one.

Telling a referrer someone declined — approved 24 Aug 2026

Emails 10 and 11 both disclose a participant's decision to someone outside our service. That was held open on the grounds that “the participant agreed to be referred” is not the same as “the participant agreed we may report their decision back”. It is now approved: the referrer has a fair reason to know, because a support coordinator has to place that person somewhere, and telling them nothing leaves the participant waiting longer.

The limit stays where it was drawn. Neither email gives a reason, and email 11 says so in as many words. What the referrer learns is the decision, not the thinking behind it.
