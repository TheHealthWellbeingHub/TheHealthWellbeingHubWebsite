What already works

A referral through the website form needs nobody to do anything for the first two steps. The records get created, the job lands with the team, the clock starts, and both emails go out — one to the referrer confirming we have it, one to the team saying someone needs to call. That part is running and confirmed reaching the right inboxes.

Where your team comes in

One place, and it should stay that way: a worker phones the participant. That is a real conversation, often the first contact a family has with us, and often in a language the worker shares with them. Nothing should automate it.

The second team step is new and much smaller — after the call, the worker tells Claude how it went. Not a form to fill in, just saying what happened in ordinary words.

What's missing

Two separate gaps, and they are different sizes.

The top of the tree. Only one of the three ways a referral arrives is automated. A referral that comes by email, phone, text or in person has no path at all — no record, no job, no clock, no acknowledgement to the person who referred them. It only enters the system if someone types it in, and if they forget, that referral is invisible.

The bottom of the tree. Once the worker has made the call, nothing captures what was said and nothing goes back to the referrer. Whoever sent us that participant never finds out what happened to them — and a referrer who never hears back stops referring.

One thing to settle before the last step is automated: telling a referrer that a participant said no shares that person's decision with someone outside our service. Often the referrer genuinely needs to know so they can help them find someone else. What we say, and what the participant has agreed we can say, is worth deciding deliberately rather than in the wording of an email.
